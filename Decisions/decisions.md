## Why the stock fell — three causes, not one narrative

### 1. Poor capex — intensity without Internet conversion

Charter spent **$11.7B** in FY2025 — **21.3% of revenue**, **51% of Adj. EBITDA**. Comcast’s cable-like Connectivity & Platforms segment spent **10.8% of C&P revenue** and **27% of C&P EBITDA**. Charter is a connectivity pure-play spending **~2×** the peer’s intensity.

Q1 2026 made the conversion failure visible in one quarter:

- Capex **$2.86B vs $2.40B** (+19%). Upgrade/rebuild **+71%** ($675M vs $395M). CPE +41%.
- Internet **−120k** and Internet *dollars* **−1.3%**. FCF **−12%**.
- Network-evolution completion is still **end-2027**. Invincible WiFi launched in February — one month of the print.

The market is not punishing “too little plant.” It is punishing plant that does not produce Internet customers. Waiting for 2027 roll-off is the base-case capital story; it does not satisfy the CEO charge by itself.

**Need to be on:** capex productivity — dollars of capital per *net Internet add* (currently negative), not miles of plant or “on-time vs 2027.” Any new spend must clear a higher bar than Comcast’s cable segment. Any deferral must name what gets cut (rural pace, evolution timing, CPE).

That is not a 24-month Internet closer.

**Hard to get out of.** Since 2022 Charter has spent **$7.7B** and targeted **>1.7M** passings, **>$8B** total, **>$2B** in RDOF / BEAD / state awards. The 10-K is explicit: miss RDOF rules and the FCC can treat Charter as in default — **penalties, forfeitures, withheld support**. Pole-permitting and subsidy milestones sit on the same clock. Sunk plant plus a ten-year subsidy operating obligation is not a program you pause because the core is bleeding.

**Need to be on:** raise take on plant already lit (38% → 54% is 221k “missing” customers on 1.385M passings) *or* stop treating leftover passings as the slope closer. Do not add rural miles to fix a core-product gap. Do not assume an exit that the subsidy contracts do not allow.

### 3. Market trends — reduced moving, switching that no longer favors cable, video lock-in gone

Three structural shifts hit at once. They are the floor (Root A). They do **not** by themselves explain why Charter’s residential Internet net-loss rate is **1.8× Comcast’s** in Q1 (0.42% vs 0.23%; excess **54k**). Industry weather plus a worse firm slope is the full story.

**Reduced moving.** The 10-K: ability to gain new customers “is dependent to some extent on growth in occupied housing.” The Team Case Brief: a slow housing market means fewer people moving and signing up. The industry’s cheapest gross-add event was a truck at a new address. That event shrank.

**Switching costs changed shape.** Traditional cable switching was painful (installer, equipment, WiFi redo), so non-movers stayed. Two things broke that:

- **FWA lowered the cost of leaving** — a self-install box, no truck, price-sensitive customers. Same carriers attacking broadband that Charter resells as an MVNO.
- **Winning a non-mover got more expensive** — if people are not changing homes, Charter has to *displace* an incumbent, not greet a mover. That is a higher cost-to-switch *in*, even as FWA made cost-to-switch *out* fall.

Those two statements are not a contradiction. They are why net adds collapsed: fewer inbound events, easier outbound for attackers.

**Reduced need for broadband TV.** Video is still large and structurally declining: FY2025 video revenue **−9.4%**; Q1 **−9.2%** and **−60k** customers (losses *moderating*, not reversing). Programmer-app bundling manages the decline. The video relationship used to raise the cost of leaving Internet. Cord-cutting and vMVPDs (YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, and the rest) removed that glue. Internet now has to win on its own.

**Need to be on:** a growth model that does not assume (a) housing-driven gross adds return, or (b) video still locks the connectivity relationship. Mobile deepens the bundle for some; it is lower-margin and the market will not credit it against broadband decline.

## Next step — broadband churn analysis = stand up (or pull) the tracker

**Do this first.** Build or pull the category breakdown they do not have, so they can see the **rate** and the **division of losses**. Every later recommendation (cut rural, retarget capex, reprice vs FWA, change the overlap offer) needs to know whether Q1 was *failed acquisition* (housing / fewer movers), *failed retention* (competitive disconnects on the core), or both — and *where*. Company net adds cannot tell them apart, and cannot steer retention vs acquisition or target overlap GTM.

### Why this is the gap

1. Charter discloses **net** Internet adds. Gross adds and gross disconnects are not in the 10-K, 10-Q, or earnings release. There is no public churn *rate*. The existing network-churn work therefore used **net-loss rate** vs Comcast (1.8×; excess 54k) as a proxy — and stated outright that gross churn is unused because it is not disclosed.
2. Field + customer-ops opex **fell $30M** YoY in Q1 while Internet losses doubled. That is evidence against a company-wide network-failure churn wave. It is **not** a substitute for a category tracker. Call-center reason codes are not that tracker (instructor: agents have favorite codes).
3. Rural **+41k** is already inside the company **−163k** CR print. Without a standing core-vs-rural *Internet* cut, rural mix will keep looking like “the company almost held” when the core lost **204k** CR. That identity is a one-off reconstruction, not a quarterly system.
4. Working assumption, to confirm with the client: **past customer data has not been tracked** in a form that supports this split. If that is wrong, the step collapses to turning the existing feed into the tracker, not building one from scratch.

#### M2. If **+mobile** is the mix at leave

**Unwilling without the data.** Whether leavers had mobile. Whether a deeper MVNO discount would have kept Internet.

#### M3. If **+video** is the mix at leave

**Response.** Unexpected if video were lock-in. Treat as whole-house disconnect: check R1 (move-out) before spending on video apps. Do not use “losses moderating” (−60k vs Comcast −322k) as connectivity relief.

**Unwilling without the data.** Mix at leave.

**Unwilling without the data.** Tenure at disconnect (explicitly “if available” on the tracker).

#### T2. If **long-life** is the leak

**Kill.** Long-life loss is move-out (aging, downsizing) → R1, not a competitive save. Do not send onboarding spend at a 10-year customer.

**Unwilling without the data.** Tenure at disconnect.
