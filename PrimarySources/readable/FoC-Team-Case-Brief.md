## Your role

Your team is a newly retained strategy consulting team engaged by the Office of the CEO of Charter Communications, Inc. (NASDAQ: CHTR), the company behind the Spectrum brand. Over seven weeks you will run a complete consulting engagement: scope the problem, analyze the business, and deliver a client-style recommendation and pitch.

Treat this as a real engagement. The facts, financials, products, and competitors in this brief are drawn from Charter’s public information as of the end of Q1 2026 (March 31, 2026). Recommendations should be grounded in that reality.

## Charter Communications, in brief

Charter is the second-largest cable operator in the United States, serving tens of millions of residential and business customers under the Spectrum brand. Core products:

- **Broadband internet (Spectrum Internet)** — the profit engine and largest driver of cash flow.
- **Video (Spectrum TV)** — large but structurally declining as customers cut the cord.
- **Mobile (Spectrum Mobile)** — newer, fast-growing service that resells wireless capacity to compete with the major carriers.
- **Voice**, plus small/medium business and enterprise services.

A transformational acquisition of Cox Communications is pending, expected to close in mid-2026, which would increase scale, footprint, and debt.

## The situation (as of end of Q1 2026)

Charter is a large, cash-generative, operationally strong company. It is also a company whose owners have lost a great deal of money.

- The stock has lost roughly 80% of its value over the past five years.
- After the Q1 2026 earnings release, the stock fell about 25% in a single day — one of the worst single-day declines in the company’s history.
- The trigger was broadband: the company lost about 120,000 internet customers in the quarter, roughly double the about 60,000 it lost in Q1 2025. Broadband is the profit engine, so subscriber losses there frighten investors more than losses anywhere else.

Why broadband is shrinking (as framed in this brief):

- Fixed wireless access (home internet over 5G from carriers such as T-Mobile and Verizon) is winning price-sensitive customers.
- Fiber overbuilders are laying fiber into Charter’s territory.
- A slow housing market means fewer people moving and signing up for new service.

The bright spot is mobile. The client added 368,000 mobile lines in the quarter and has grown its mobile base by roughly 17% over the past year — but mobile is a lower-margin, resold service, and investors have not been willing to credit it against the broadband decline.

The company still produces substantial revenue (about $13.6 billion in the quarter) and strong free cash flow, and carries a large debt load that the pending acquisition will increase.

The company is not in financial distress. Its problem is direction and belief: the market does not believe the current strategy will return the business to growth, and the share price reflects that loss of confidence.

## The CEO’s charge to your team

> “I need you to bring me out-of-the-box strategies and business moves that could genuinely change the trajectory of our share price. Tell me what we should do — and then convince me we can actually do it. What would it take, what would it cost, and how long would it take to matter?”

The mandate is deliberately open. There is no predetermined right answer, and “keep doing what we’re doing, but better” is unlikely to satisfy the client. Bold, creative, and unconventional moves are encouraged. The recommendation must be the team’s own, and it must be defensible and actionable.

## What we are asking you to deliver

- **Frame the problem.** Separate the symptom (a falling stock price) from the underlying business problem. Define, precisely, what problem you are actually solving.
- **Analyze the business and its market.** Use the data and evidence available to you (including a data pack provided in Week 5) to build a fact base and test hypotheses.
- **Recommend a course of action.** A clear, specific recommendation for how the client should change its trajectory, and why it is the right move under real-world uncertainty.
- **Show it can be done.** Feasibility: resources, capabilities, time, money, and risks.
- **Present to the client.** A final, client-style PowerPoint as if presenting to the CEO.

## Ground rules on the recommendation

- **Be specific.** “Invest in growth” is not a recommendation. “Do X, targeting Y, funded by Z, over N months” is.
- **Own the trade-offs.** Every real strategic move costs something or closes off something else. Name it.
- **Anchor to the facts as of end of Q1 2026.** You may reference later public developments for additional context, but they must be documented, publicly available facts from reputable sources.
