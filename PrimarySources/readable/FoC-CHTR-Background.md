## 1. Executive snapshot

Charter Communications is a leading U.S. broadband connectivity and cable operator that sells Internet, mobile, video, and voice services under the Spectrum brand. As of December 31, 2025 its network passed 58 million homes and businesses across 41 states, and it employed roughly 91,900 full-time-equivalent employees, all U.S.-based.

## 2. Company overview, footprint, and strategy

### History and identity

Charter was founded in 1993 and has evolved from a traditional cable-TV operator into what it describes as "a leading broadband connectivity company." Its offering has shifted from cable TV and high-speed Internet toward a converged broadband, WiFi, and mobile experience delivered over what the company calls its Spectrum Fiber Broadband Network (also described in the filings as its "fiber-powered network"). All consumer and business services are marketed under the Spectrum family of brands (Spectrum, Spectrum Business, Spectrum Reach, Spectrum Community Solutions).

Charter's principal executive offices are at 400 Washington Blvd., Stamford, Connecticut. It is incorporated in Delaware and trades on Nasdaq under the ticker CHTR.

### Footprint

The network is available to 58 million homes and small-to-large businesses across 41 states. Charter operates in geographically diverse areas managed centrally on a consolidated basis. It is the second-largest cable operator in the United States (behind Comcast), but will be the largest once the pending deal to merge with Cox Communications is completed.

### Stated strategy

Charter frames its strategy around using its fiber-powered network to deliver high-quality, competitively priced products with strong service, so it can (1) grow the number of customers on the network and (2) grow the number of products sold to each customer. The logic it articulates: more products per relationship reduces service transactions per customer, which raises satisfaction and lowers churn, which in turn lowers the cost to acquire and serve customers and drives profitability.

Two consumer-facing pillars support this:

"Life Unlimited" brand platform, emphasizing the network and connectivity products.

Simplified pricing and packaging, offering lower promotional and persistent bundled pricing (Internet + Advanced WiFi + Unlimited Mobile) to drive growth, together with customer commitments on reliability, transparency, and service.

### Corporate structure and ownership

Charter Communications, Inc. is the publicly traded parent; the operating businesses sit under Charter Communications Holdings, LLC ("Charter Holdings") and its subsidiaries (including CCO Holdings, LLC and Charter Communications Operating, LLC, the primary debt issuers). Historically, Liberty Broadband and the Advance/Newhouse Partnership ("A/N") have been Charter's largest shareholders — a relationship now being restructured through the pending Liberty Broadband Combination and Cox Transactions (Section 9).

## 3. Products and services

Charter sells subscription-based Internet, mobile, video, and voice services to residential and commercial customers, individually or as bundles. Bundled services are available to substantially all passings.

### Residential connectivity

Spectrum Internet. Speeds up to 1 Gbps are available across the entire footprint, with multi-gigabit speeds in a portion of it. Charter is upgrading its network to deliver symmetrical and multi-gigabit speeds footprint-wide over the next several years (see Section 6, Network evolution). Advanced WiFi, a managed in-home WiFi service using WiFi 7 routers, is available to all Internet customers and adds features such as Security Shield and parental controls. In early 2026 Charter planned to launch Invincible WiFi, a tri-band WiFi 7 router integrating 5G cellular and battery backup to keep customers connected during outages.

Spectrum Mobile. Available to all new and existing Spectrum Internet customers. Plans include 5G, require no contracts, and include taxes and fees in the price. Mobile runs over a combination of (a) customers' private WiFi, (b) Charter's ~49 million out-of-home WiFi access points plus partner WiFi, and (c) Verizon's cellular network (Charter is an MVNO on Verizon). In July 2025 Charter signed a multi-year agreement with T-Mobile to carry mobile services for Spectrum Business customers, launching in 2026. Product integrations such as Speed Boost tie the mobile and fixed-Internet products together.

Spectrum Voice. Wireline voice over VoIP, including unlimited domestic-plus-Canada/Mexico/Puerto Rico calling and features such as Call Guard robocall blocking.

### Residential video

Charter provides roughly 375 channels plus on-demand and streaming. It has renegotiated affiliation agreements with major programmers to include ad-supported versions of programmer streaming apps at no extra cost inside certain Spectrum TV packages, with à-la-carte and ad-free upgrade options (and, since October 2025, a Spectrum App Store). New video customers receive Xumo stream boxes (a Charter/Comcast joint venture device), now the preferred go-to-market platform for new video sales. The Spectrum TV app, cloud DVR, and ~100,000 VOD titles round out the offering.

### Commercial services

Spectrum Business (small business): Internet, mobile, video, and voice over the fiber-powered network, with speeds up to 1 Gbps, plus business features (static IP, hosted voice, wireless Internet backup).

Spectrum Enterprise (mid-market & large / enterprise): tailored connectivity and managed services over a high-capacity last-mile network with speeds up to 100 Gbps, serving large businesses, government, and wholesale carrier customers (Ethernet, SD-WAN, cloud connectivity, managed security, voice trunking, video solutions for hospitality/healthcare/government/education).

### Advertising — Spectrum Reach

Sells local, regional, and national advertising across cable networks, streaming/FAST channels, and advanced/addressable platforms; inserts local ads on up to 100 channels in 90+ markets; and represents other distributors' inventory through interconnects.

### Other services

Regional sports networks (Spectrum SportsNet with Lakers rights through 2032; affiliate/ad relationship with SportsNet LA carrying the Dodgers; 35% stake in SportsNet New York carrying the Mets).

News channels: 35+ local news channels including Spectrum News NY1 and Spectrum News SoCal.

Spectrum Community Solutions: broadband to MDUs, gated communities, student and senior housing, and RV parks (Internet up to 2 Gbps).

### What the customer base tells us

Internet is the profit engine and it is shrinking. Charter lost 403,000 total Internet customers in 2025 (a 1.3% decline). This is the single most important operating trend for investors because Internet carries the highest margins and the most pricing power.

Mobile is the growth engine. Mobile lines grew nearly 1.9 million (+19%) in 2025 to 11.8 million. Mobile is comparatively lower-margin (it resells network capacity), but it deepens bundles and is central to Charter's convergence strategy.

Video and voice are in secular decline. Video fell 287,000 (cord-cutting) and voice fell 838,000 (wireless substitution). Video remains a large revenue line but a shrinking one.

ARPU is roughly flat. Monthly residential revenue per customer was essentially unchanged at ~$119, reflecting Charter's deliberate move to lower, simpler promotional and bundled pricing to defend the base.

Bad-debt aging improved. Accounts over 60 days past due fell to ~82,300 (from ~102,500), a modest positive signal on collections.

### Business (commercial) customers

Charter serves ~2.2 million small-business relationships and, separately, mid-market & large-business customers measured in PSUs (357,000 fiber service offerings at year-end 2025, up 5%). Commercial is a smaller but steadier and growing part of the mix (Section 8).

## 5. Operating nuances

Several structural features of how Charter operates are important context for any strategic analysis.

Reporting-methodology change (important for trend analysis). In Q4 2025 Charter revised its customer statistics to include all mobile customers (including mobile-only) and to add "total connectivity customers," and it standardized some small-business and mid-market definitions. Prior periods were restated. Consequence: customer counts pulled from the FY2024 10-K (old basis) differ slightly from the restated FY2024 figures in the FY2025 10-K. For example, FY2024 residential customer relationships appear as 29,258 (old) vs. 29,964 (restated). This document uses the restated figures from the FY2025 10-K for 2024–2025 and flags 2023 (old basis) where used. Students building trend charts should not mix the two bases without a footnote.

Insourced field and service operations. Charter runs a 100% U.S.-based workforce and performs most work in-house: in 2025 its in-house field operations handled over 80% of customer premise service transactions, and it has been growing in-house construction crews for network expansion. Management argues this drives consistent, higher-quality service and lower churn. It also means a large fixed labor base (~91,900 FTEs).

Human capital. Minimum starting wage of at least $20/hour; ~80% of employees eligible for variable comp; 401(k) with dollar-for-dollar match up to 6%; tuition-free degree program and a Department of Labor–certified Broadband Technician Apprenticeship. Labor is both a differentiator and a major cost lever.

Programming economics. Charter licenses video programming (usually per-subscriber fees) from a consolidating set of suppliers with growing leverage. Charter's recent programmer deals bundling streaming apps into video packages are an attempt to restore value and stabilize the video product.

Seasonality and cyclicality. Results are seasonal (college/vacation service areas) and cyclical (advertising is stronger in Q2/Q4 and in even-numbered years due to political advertising). Capital expenditure and working capital are also seasonal, tied to construction and subscriber timing.

## 6. Network, technology, and network evolution

Network architecture. Three components: a national IP backbone, regional/metro networks, and a "last-mile" network that is largely hybrid fiber-coaxial (HFC) — fiber to the node, coax to the home — which Charter brands as its "fiber-powered network." New builds (rural and MDU) are typically all-fiber. Mid-market/large-business sites get fiber to the premises.

Network evolution initiative (the big capital story). Charter is upgrading the HFC plant to deliver symmetrical, multi-gigabit speeds using: spectrum expansion to 1.2 GHz then 1.8 GHz; "high-split" bandwidth reallocation for faster upstream; Distributed Access Architecture (DAA); and DOCSIS 4.0. Management expects this to be largely complete by the end of 2027. The upgrade is designed to answer fiber and fixed-wireless competition at a lower incremental cost than overbuilding with fiber.

WiFi and mobile infrastructure. ~500 million devices connect to Charter's network in customer homes/businesses; Charter is deploying WiFi 7 routers and building targeted 5G small cells using its 210 CBRS Priority Access Licenses plus its HFC network for backhaul/power, to offload mobile traffic onto owned infrastructure and improve mobile economics.

Subsidized rural construction initiative. Since inception in early 2022 Charter has spent $7.7 billion and activated ~1.3 million passings, targeting over 1.7 million passings. It expects to invest over $8 billion total, partially offset by government subsidies (over $2 billion awarded to date via RDOF, BEAD, and other grants). Management frames rural builds as "infrastructure-style returns." Execution depends on external factors like utility-pole permitting, and subsidy programs carry build-out milestones with financial penalties for misses.

## 7. Competitive landscape

Charter faces intense, intensifying competition across every product.

Internet. The two structural threats:

Fiber-to-the-home (FTTH) overbuilders — primarily AT&T and Verizon — now offer 1 Gbps+ speeds overlapping Charter's footprint. Per the FY2025 10-K, Charter faces terrestrial 100 Mbps+ competition from AT&T in ~27% of its footprint and from Verizon in ~16%.

Fixed wireless access (FWA) — national mobile operators (notably T-Mobile and Verizon) delivering 5G home Internet from cell towers, winning price-sensitive customers.

Plus DSL, satellite, municipal WiFi, and open-access networks.

Mobile. Charter competes against the national MNOs — AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile — and other MVNOs/resellers, all of which are also pushing converged connectivity bundles similar to Charter's.

Video. Growing competition from virtual MVPDs (YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, Sling, Philo, DirecTV Stream), SVOD/streaming apps (Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, Peacock, Paramount+, etc.), free ad-supported streaming, DBS satellite providers, and Verizon's wireline video. Piracy and password sharing also erode the base.

Voice / advertising / commercial. Voice competes with wireless, OTT, and messaging; advertising competes across broadcast, digital, social, and connected-TV platforms; commercial competes with fiber carriers, cloud/managed-service providers, and other telcos.

The strategic picture: Charter is simultaneously defending broadband against fiber + FWA, monetizing mobile as an MVNO against the same carriers that attack its broadband, and managing a structurally declining video business.

## 10. Strategic transactions in flight

Two large, interrelated transactions were pending as of the most recent filings and materially change Charter's future scale, ownership, and balance sheet.

Cox Transactions (announced May 16, 2025). Charter agreed to combine with Cox Communications (residential cable) and acquire Cox's commercial fiber and managed IT/cloud businesses. Consideration includes $3.5 billion cash for the equity sale, plus (in the FY2025 10-K) $500 million cash for the contribution, $6.0 billion of convertible preferred units (6.875% dividend, $477.41 conversion price), ~33.6 million Charter Holdings common units, and one share of newly created Class C stock. The combined entity would assume ~$12.6 billion of Cox net debt (per the FY2025 10-K). Cox Enterprises would become a major long-term shareholder. (Note: the Q1 2026 10-Q updates two of these figures — see Section 11.)

Liberty Broadband Combination (announced November 12, 2024). Charter agreed to acquire Liberty Broadband, whose principal asset is ~41.5 million Charter shares, simplifying Charter's ownership structure (Liberty exchange ratio 0.236 Charter Class A shares per Liberty share). Liberty first spun off its GCI Alaska business (completed July 14, 2025). The two deals are expected to close contemporaneously.

Strategically, these transactions increase Charter's scale and footprint, consolidate its ownership, and add debt — raising the stakes on the same broadband-vs-competition question the core business already faces.

### Bottom line on Q1 2026

The quarter crystallized the bear case: the profit engine (Internet) kept shrinking, EBITDA turned negative year-over-year, and capex/free-cash-flow moved the wrong way — even as mobile grew and video/voice losses moderated. That combination is what a team advising the CEO on "changing the trajectory" must grapple with.

## 12. Implications for the case (neutral framing)

Without prescribing an answer, the filings surface the tensions a strategy team will need to define and address:

The core asset is a cash-generative but slow/declining connectivity business carrying heavy leverage and heavy capital needs.

Broadband erosion is driven by two durable competitive forces (fiber overbuild + fixed wireless) that a network upgrade may blunt but not eliminate.

Mobile growth is real but lower-margin and dependent on MVNO economics (Verizon today, T-Mobile for business from 2026) and on Charter's own 5G/CBRS offload strategy.

Video is structurally declining but still large; the programmer-app bundling strategy is an attempt to manage the decline, not reverse it.

Capital allocation has leaned heavily on buybacks to convert flat earnings into EPS growth; the pending Cox and Liberty transactions reset scale, ownership, and leverage.

The market's loss of confidence (reflected in the share price) is a symptom; the underlying question is whether Charter can credibly return connectivity relationships to growth, and over what horizon and at what cost.
