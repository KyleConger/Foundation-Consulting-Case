## 1. The number, and the decision

**Commit — US annual market size (TAM): ≈ $1.25B per year.** Range $1.0B–$1.7B. That is the
**United States only**, **per year**, for insulated bottles and tumblers at a **realized** price of
$35–$50. No global or North American total is carried into this figure anywhere in the build.

| Build | US annual TAM, $35–$50 band |
|---|---:|
| **Top-down** (US population → owners → annual purchases → band units × ASP) | **$1,232M per year** |
| **Bottom-up** (US brand-level annual retail → band share) | **$1,327M per year** |
| **Committed, rounded down** | **$1,250M per year** |

The two builds land within 7.5% of each other, but they are **not independent** — see the flag in §2.3.

**Realistically achievable for her: ≈ $4.4M revenue by year 3** (range $3.0M–$6.0M), about
**128,000 units** at a blended **$34.60**. That is **0.35% of the TAM**.

**Recommendation: go — but not as a direct-to-consumer consumer brand.** Enter as a
**B2B-led decorated hybrid**, sourced outside China, with Amazon as the consumer beachhead and a
cleanability-led product claim. The consumer path at $35–$50 does not fund its own customer
acquisition; the corporate channel does.

The honest read: this is a **$4.4M niche operating business**, not a venture-scale outcome, unless the
product claim proves genuinely defensible. If she raised seed money against a Stanley-style narrative,
the sizing says that story is not available anymore.

## 3. What she can actually address (SAM)

Frame 2 says she has no brand and therefore no shelf for 24–36 months. That removes mass retail from
the addressable set and splits the opportunity in two.

| Addressable pool | Size ($M) | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Consumer, online only (Amazon + own DTC) | 525 | 42% of TAM sold online | Low-Medium |
| ↳ narrowed to replacement/addition purchases (75%) | 394 | Frame 1 | Low |
| ↳ narrowed to hygiene/cleanability trigger (40%) | **158** | Frame 1 | **Low** |
| B2B decorated, supplier-level premium insulated | **681** | Derived from ASI promo data | Medium-Low |
| **Combined realistic SAM** | **≈ 840** | | Low-Medium |

B2B derivation: North American promotional products distributor sales were a record **$27.7B in 2025**
(+4.2%, ASI). Drinkware is the **#2 category at roughly 10.5%** of industry sales ≈ **$2.91B**
distributor-level. Taking 90% US, 40% premium insulated stainless, and 65% supplier-level pricing gives
**$681M** of annual demand she could quote into.

**This is the finding that should change her plan.** The B2B pool is **4.3× larger** than the consumer
niche her framing points at, and it is the one channel where the buying criteria are spec, decoration,
minimum order quantity, and delivery reliability rather than brand equity. Corroborating signal: ASI
reported that **about 90% of promo distributors raised prices in 2025 by an average of 11%** to offset
China and India import costs — this channel passes duty through, which the consumer band does not.

## 4. Unit economics — why the channel choice is forced

### 4.1 Landed cost by origin (per unit, $10.00 FOB basis)

| Line | China | Vietnam | India |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FOB | 10.00 | 10.50 | 10.00 |
| Freight + insurance | 1.20 | 1.20 | 1.30 |
| CIF | 11.20 | 11.70 | 11.30 |
| Duty rate applied | **44.5%** | **19.5%** | **17.0%** |
| Duty | 4.98 | 2.28 | 1.92 |
| MPF and broker fees | 0.08 | 0.08 | 0.08 |
| **Landed cost** | **16.26** | **14.06** | **13.30** |
| Saving vs China | — | **2.20** | **2.96** |

China stack: ~7% MFN on HTS 9617.00.1000 + 25% Section 301 + 12.5% Section 301 forced-labor duty
effective July 24, 2026. Vietnam and India carry the MFN rate plus 12.5% and 10% respectively under the
same July 2026 action. **Confidence: Medium — must be confirmed by a licensed customs broker before
pricing.**

### 4.2 Contribution by channel (Vietnam sourcing, $14.06 landed)

| Channel | Price | Net after fees | Contribution | Margin | Media CAC | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| Own DTC, paid media | 40.00 | 31.20 | 17.14 | 42.9% | 25–40 | **Negative on first order** |
| Amazon FBA | 40.00 | 27.20 | 13.14 | 32.9% | ~7.20 PPC | Thin but positive (~15%) |

At $35–$50 retail, blended cold-traffic customer acquisition cost exceeds per-unit contribution. Paid

**Do not fund a paid-media DTC launch.**

## 5. Realistic three-year path (SOM)

All revenue lines are units × the **$34.60** blended ASP from §4.3. Share of TAM is measured against
the committed **US annual** TAM of $1,250M.

| Year | Units | Revenue | Mix | Share of US annual TAM |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 15,000 | $0.52M | B2B pilots + Amazon listing | 0.04% |

Range on year 3: **$3.0M–$6.0M**. Confidence: **Low-Medium** — driven by how many corporate accounts

($2.36M) plus 0.3% of the $680.6M B2B pool ($2.04M) = **$4.40M**, within 0.7% of the units-times-price
result. Break-even at 41,000 units is crossed during year 2.

## 7. Actions, sequenced by cost of resolution

| # | Action | Cost | Days | Kills or changes the plan if |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Customs broker ruling on HTS 9617.00.1000, **and** whether vacuum flasks are caught by the 50% Section 232 steel-derivative duty | $1–2K | 5–10 | A 232 finding adds ~$4/unit and invalidates $35–$50 |
| 2 | Quote Vietnam, India, Korea factories; target FOB ≤ $10.50, tooling ≤ $35K | $0 | 10–20 | No factory meets FOB at her MOQ |
| 3 | Freedom-to-operate search on lid and silhouette | $3–6K | 10–15 | Her form reads onto a live design patent |
| 4 | Batch-level FDA 21 CFR and Prop 65 testing | $2–4K | 10–15 | Coatings or lid fail; claims unsupportable |
| 5 | 20 corporate buyer conversations in ASI's top promo verticals (education, healthcare, construction, manufacturing, financial) at $28–34 decorated, 250–500 MOQ | ~$0 | 20–30 | Fewer than 3 of 20 will pilot |
| 6 | Single Amazon listing with the cleanability claim; measure organic conversion and PPC efficiency before scaling | $15–25K | 30–45 | PPC cost per acquisition exceeds contribution |

**Kill rule, stated in advance:** if landed cost cannot get below $16, **and** there is no defensible
cleanability claim, **and** fewer than 3 of 20 corporate buyers will pilot at $28 or more, then do not
scale. Pivot to licensing or contract supply and preserve the remaining seed capital.

## 8. What we are unwilling to claim

- That the $1.25B TAM is measured. It is triangulated from two builds with several judgment inputs.
- That the ownership and replacement percentages are reliable. The 85%-own / 51%-replace survey was
  **commissioned by a bottle startup** and needs independent replication before it drives a product roadmap.
- That the duty stack is final. Trade policy moved twice in 2026; the Section 232 question is open.
- That she has a right to win at all. The case says only that she has a product. **Frame 2 cannot be
  closed by research — only by naming the asset she holds that a competitor cannot buy or copy.**

## Appendix B — The prompt, extended for Practice B

The Practice A prompt asked for framings. This one has to ask for a **number tied to those framings**,
with the decision named. Built on Sean's six controls.

> **Role.** You are an action-oriented market-sizing consultant and decision maker, not a summarizer.
> You will commit to a single number and defend it.
>
> **Context.** The client is a founder entering the US market for premium insulated drinkware, bottles
> and tumblers at $35–$50 retail. She has a finished product, some seed funding, and no market
> analysis. We have already framed her problem three ways: displacement of a vessel already owned;
> founder-specific right-to-win, valid only if it names an asset a competitor cannot buy or copy; and
> landed cost plus duty stack. The audience is the founder, who must decide whether and how to commit
> the remaining seed capital.
>
> **Task.** Produce (a) a committed annual US market size for the $35–$50 realized-price band, built
> both top-down and bottom-up and reconciled; (b) the serviceable subset implied by each of the three
> frames; (c) a realistic three-year revenue and unit target with the share of market it represents;
> (d) per-unit landed cost by country of origin and contribution by channel; (e) a recommendation on
> where she should go, with a sequenced action list and a kill rule stated in advance.
>
> **Constraints.** Use SEC filings and government statistics where they exist, and say so. Every
> assumption on its own line with a value, a confidence rating, and a source status. Flag every figure
> you are not confident in, and name who paid for any survey you rely on. Where the case is
> deliberately ambiguous, state the ambiguity and commit anyway. Do not hedge into ranges without also
> giving a point estimate. Do not invent customer data. Explicitly state what you are unwilling to claim.
>
> **Format.** A decision memo with markdown tables, plus a machine-readable assumption register as CSV.
> Lead with the number and the recommendation, not the method.
>
> **Example of the granularity required.** "US insulated drinkware retail, all price points: $3,790M
> (Low-Medium confidence, derived). Of which YETI $1,090M (Medium, derived from filed FY2025 drinkware
> net sales of $1,085.8M and US share of 78.9%)."

**What changed from Practice A, and why it matters:** the prompt now (1) forces a point estimate rather
than a range, (2) makes each frame produce its own serviceable-market number so the framing does
analytical work instead of sitting decoratively on top of it, (3) requires channel-level contribution,
because the sizing is meaningless if no channel can profitably serve the market, and (4) demands a kill
rule up front so the analysis cannot be quietly rationalized into a yes.
